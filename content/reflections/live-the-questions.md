Rainer Maria Rilke, writing to a young poet who wanted certainty, offered him something else instead: patience.

> *"Be patient toward all that is unsolved in your heart and try to love the questions themselves... Do not now seek the answers, which cannot be given you because you would not be able to live them. And the point is, to live everything. Live the questions now."*

I've built my career around answers — a control loop either stabilizes or it doesn't, a battery either lasts 2,000 cycles or it doesn't, code either passes verification or it fails. Engineering rewards certainty, and I'm good at chasing it.

But not everything worth thinking about resolves that cleanly. *Why build anything at all? What do I owe the people affected by the systems I design? What does a good life actually look like, once the resume is impressive?*

I don't have tidy answers to those, and I've stopped expecting to. What I've started doing instead is treating the questions as companions rather than problems — carrying them from one project, one city, one year to the next, and letting them change shape as I do.

**Live the questions.** Not because the answers don't matter, but because the living — done honestly, attentively, one uncertain day at a time — is usually where the answer was hiding anyway.
