I am an engineer by profession, with an interest in philosophy. I like to examine life, create things, challenge established ideas, and in the process, discover myself.

Descartes stripped away everything he could doubt — his senses, his body, the world itself — until only one thing remained standing: the fact that he was doubting at all. *Cogito, ergo sum.* I think, therefore I am.

I find that oddly practical, for something so abstract. To exist requires us to think, and thinking, in turn, gives us our existence. Not a static fact to file away, but something closer to a verb — a thing you keep doing, deliberately, or you drift.

Most days that looks like ordinary curiosity: taking a system apart to see why it behaves the way it does, questioning a design decision everyone else has stopped questioning, sitting with an idea long enough to see if it survives scrutiny. Examining life the way I'd examine a circuit — not to reduce it, but to actually understand it before I decide what I believe about it.

I don't think philosophy and engineering pull in different directions. Both start from the same discipline: don't accept a conclusion you haven't tested. Both end, if you let them, at the same quiet insistence — *think, and you will find yourself still there.*
